What it does

A scout, not another inbox.

InboxScout doesn't replace your email app. It reads alongside it and reports back in plain language.

📬

Reads all your mail, read-only

Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud, Outlook.com, Hotmail, any IMAP mailbox — several at once. It can never send, delete, or move anything.

🧠

Sorts personal from work

Plus what needs a reply, what's a newsletter, what's a receipt — and a quiet heads-up when something contains sensitive information.

🔎

Watches for what matters to you

Bills, appointments, deals, work orders, deliveries, travel, school, health, job search, customer requests, the people you care about. Tick what fits.

👥

Knows your people and your week

Learns who matters across every inbox, tells you when someone has gone quiet, gathers the week's dates from all your accounts, and remembers what you promised.

📨

Comes to you

Email yourself the brief, get the headline as a text message, or have it read out loud when it's ready. It only ever sends to you.

✍

Helps you act

One click opens a reply draft in your own mail app (you send it), adds dates to your calendar, or saves briefs to Google Docs and Sheets.

🩹

Fixes itself

Checks its own health on every run and quietly repairs what it safely can. When something really needs you, it says so in plain words, with one button: Fix it for me.

🤖

Does the fiddly parts for you

An assistant opens its own browser window and handles the setup chores — app passwords, connecting accounts — or any web task you describe. There's always a Stop button.

Free & private

Works out of the box. Nothing leaves your computer unless you say so.

  • Local-first. Your mail lives in a database on your computer. Passwords and keys are locked with your system's own keychain.
  • No AI account required. A built-in engine sorts, tracks, and writes briefs entirely on your PC. Connect a smarter AI later if you like.
  • Read-only, always. InboxScout can't send, delete, or move your mail. Replies are drafts you send yourself.
  • You choose how far it goes. The assistant asks before it uses your computer; one switch turns it off. Every setting says what it does and why.
  • Source-available. The code is public on GitHub, so anyone can check what it does.

Install help

First-time install tips

Windows says “Windows protected your PC”

InboxScout isn't code-signed yet (that costs money; we're in the friends-and-family stage). Click More info → Run anyway. It's a one-time message; the app updates itself after that.

Mac says “InboxScout can't be opened”

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway, then open InboxScout again. One time only.

Which Mac download do I need?

Apple menu → About This Mac. If you see “Apple M1/M2/M3/M4”, choose Apple Silicon; if you see “Intel”, choose Intel.

How does it connect to Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud, or Outlook?

Gmail and Outlook can use a normal “Sign in with Google / Microsoft” page. Gmail, Yahoo, and iCloud also work with an app password — a special 16-character password just for InboxScout. The setup screen shows exactly where to get one, or the built-in assistant can create it for you.

Is it really free?

Yes. The app, the built-in engine, text messages through your carrier's free gateway, updates — all free. The only optional cost is a paid AI service you might choose to connect.

Can I set it up for a parent or grandparent?

Yes — that's one of the main reasons it exists. Install it, connect their email, pick the Simple layout (or let it pick), and turn on “Read it to me.” They get one big button and three cards.
