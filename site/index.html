How it works

Two minutes to set up. Thirty seconds a day after that.

STEP 1

Connect your email

Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud, Outlook.com/Hotmail, or any other mailbox. Read-only — InboxScout can never send, delete, or change anything.

STEP 2

Say what you do — or don't

Nurse, contractor, teacher, landlord, retiree, developer, pastor, caregiver… 56 kinds of people. Pick one, or let InboxScout figure it out from your mail. It tunes what it watches for.

STEP 3

Get your brief

Every morning (or whenever you press the button): what needs you, what you're waiting on, what's coming up — or simply "you're all caught up."

What it does

A scout, not another inbox.

InboxScout doesn't replace your email app. It reads alongside it and reports back in plain language.

🩹

Fixes itself

InboxScout checks its own health every time it runs and quietly repairs what it safely can — reconnecting an account, falling back to the built-in engine when an AI helper fails, finding a new home for your reports. You see one calm line about what it did. When something really needs you, it says so in plain words, with one button: Fix it for me.

🧠

Sorts personal from work

Plus what needs a reply, what's a newsletter, what's a receipt — and a quiet heads-up when something contains sensitive information (never hidden, never redacted).

🔎

Watches for what matters to you

Tick the "skills" that fit your life: bills & invoices, appointments, real-estate deals, work orders & compliance, deliveries, travel, school & family, health, job search, customer requests, important people.

📰

Daily or weekly brief

Top issues with a concrete next step each, a rolling pulse on your projects or deals, a reply tracker, and upcoming dates. Saved as HTML, PDF, and Markdown.

📨

Comes to you

Email yourself the brief, get the headline as a text message, or have InboxScout read it out loud when it's ready. It only ever sends to you.

✍

Helps you act

One click opens a reply draft in your own mail app (you send it), adds dates to your calendar, exports a list, or saves briefs to Google Docs and Sheets.

🧩

Extensible for teams

Companies add their own watchers as small JSON files and can route matching mail to their own AI agents. Simple for people, powerful underneath.

🎚

Simple for a grandparent, sharp for a CEO

The layout grows with you: one big button and three cards with spoken help, or a dense decisions-first view across every inbox. InboxScout picks from how you use it, tells you when it changes, and lets you lock it. Every setting says what it does and why.

👥

Knows your people and your week

Learns who matters across every inbox and treats their mail as important on its own. Tells you when someone's gone quiet, gathers the week's dates from all your accounts (and spots overlaps), and remembers what you promised people. Nothing to set up.

🤖

An assistant that does the fiddly parts

It opens its own browser window and creates app passwords, connects accounts, or does any web chore you describe. Save a sign-in and it logs in for you — the AI never sees the password. You pick how far it goes; there's always a Stop button.

🖥

Uses your whole computer — and asks first

The assistant can open apps and files, click and type on your desktop, run commands, and work with files in your home folder. The first time it does each kind of thing, a popup asks you: allow once, always, or never. Dangerous things ask too, unless you keep the full-autonomy choice on (it is on by default; turn it off in Settings → Who can help). One switch turns it all off.

🤝

Works with Hermes and other agents

Turn on the local Agent bridge and InboxScout becomes a tool for Hermes, Claude Code, or any MCP client — read the brief, search mail, connect accounts, drive the assistant. Read-only or full access, your choice.

Free & private

Works out of the box. Nothing leaves your computer unless you say so.

  • No AI account required. A built-in engine sorts, tracks, and writes briefs entirely on your PC.
  • Optional smarter AI. Connect a free tier (Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter, Mistral) or a local model (Ollama, LM Studio) in one click — or a paid one if you prefer.
  • Local-first. Your mail lives in a local database; passwords and keys are encrypted with your OS keychain.
  • Read-only, always. InboxScout can't send, delete, or move your mail. Replies are drafts you send yourself.
  • Source-available. The code is public on GitHub under the FSL-1.1-MIT license.

Install help

First-time install tips

Windows says "Windows protected your PC"

InboxScout isn't code-signed yet (that costs money; we're in the friends-and-family stage). Click More info → Run anyway. It's a one-time message; the app updates itself after that.

Mac says "InboxScout can't be opened"

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway, then open InboxScout again. One time only.

Which Mac download do I need?

Apple menu → About This Mac. If you see "Apple M1/M2/M3/M4", choose Apple Silicon; if you see "Intel", choose Intel.

How does it connect to Gmail / Yahoo / iCloud / Outlook?

Gmail and Outlook can use a normal "Sign in with Google / Microsoft" page. Gmail, Yahoo, and iCloud also work with an app password — a special 16-character password just for InboxScout; the setup screen shows exactly where to get one.

Is it really free?

Yes. The app, the built-in engine, texts via your carrier's free gateway, updates — all free. The only optional costs are paid AI backends you might choose to connect.
